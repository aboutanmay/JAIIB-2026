## 1. The Journal (Book of Original Entry)

**The Core Rule:** The Journal is the **Original Book of Entry** (or Book of Prime Entry). This is where transactions are recorded for the very first time, directly from the Source Documents.

**Key Features & Keyword Mapping:**
- **Record Type:** It is a **Chronological Record**. Transactions are recorded sequentially, strictly by date and time of occurrence.
- **Process Name:** Recording a transaction in the Journal is called **"Journalizing"**.
- **Narration:** A brief explanation of the transaction written immediately below the entry in the Journal.
- **L.F. (Ledger Folio):** A column in the Journal where the *page number of the Ledger* is written. This is used for quick cross-referencing and verification.

---

## 2. The Ledger (Book of Final Entry)

**The Core Rule:** The Ledger is the **Book of Final Entry**. It groups or classifies transactions of a similar nature into one place (e.g., grouping all cash transactions into the Cash Account).

> **Case Scenario (The Need for a Ledger):** A French fries cart owner receives varying orders from a customer (Alia) every day. To track how much she owes, he opens a specific "Alia Account" and logs dates, amounts, and payments there. This individual summary account is a **Ledger**.

**Key Features & Keyword Mapping:**
- **Record Type:** It is an **Analytical Record**. It requires analysis to determine which account (and which side: Debit or Credit) the transaction belongs to.
- **Format:** Every Ledger account is **"T-shaped"**. The left side is Debit, and the right side is Credit.
- **Process Name:** Transferring a transaction from the Journal to the Ledger is called **"Posting"**.
- **J.F. (Journal Folio):** A column in the Ledger where the *page number of the Journal* is written to trace where the entry came from.

---

## 3. Comparison: Journal vs. Ledger (High-Yield for MCQs)

| Feature | Journal | Ledger |
|---------|---------|--------|
| **Entry Type** | Book of **Original/First** Entry | Book of **Final** Entry |
| **Record Type** | **Chronological** (Sequence by date) | **Analytical** (Grouped by nature) |
| **Process** | **Journalizing** | **Posting** |
| **Folio Column** | Contains **L.F.** (Ledger Folio page) | Contains **J.F.** (Journal Folio page) |
| **Reliability** | **More Reliable** | **Less Reliable** |

> **MCQ Trap (Reliability):** "The Ledger is more reliable than the Journal because it summarizes data." -> **FALSE**. The **Journal is MORE reliable**. 
> *Why?* Because the Journal is created directly from verified Source Documents. The Ledger is manually copied ("posted") from the Journal, and clerical errors (e.g., copying ₹540 as ₹450) frequently happen during posting.

## MCQs Solved in the Video

**Q1.** An accountant accidentally posts a journal entry in the wrong ledger account. What does this imply about the reliability of the books?
**Answer: The Ledger becomes unreliable.**
*Explanation:* Posting errors (copying mistakes from Journal to Ledger) are common, which is why the Ledger is considered strictly *less reliable* than the Journal.

**Q2.** Which one of the following best explains why the Journal is considered more reliable than the Ledger?
**Answer: Because Journal transactions are supported by Source Documents.**
*Explanation:* The Journal is the first point of entry directly backed by hard evidence (invoices, receipts).

**Q3.** Which of the following statements about Journal and Ledger is **NOT** correct?
(A) The term "Posting" means transferring items from Journal to Ledger.
(B) Journal is a book of chronological record.
(C) Ledger is a book of analytical record.
(D) Ledger is the book of first entry.
**Answer: (D) Ledger is the book of first entry.**
*Why Option D is wrong:* The Ledger is the book of **Final** entry. The Journal is the book of **First** entry.

## Quick-Fire Revision

- **Journal:** Original Entry | Chronological | Journalizing | More Reliable.
- **Ledger:** Final Entry | Analytical | Posting | Less Reliable (prone to posting errors).
- **Narration:** Short description below a journal entry.
- **L.F. (Ledger Folio):** Column in Journal pointing to the Ledger page.
- **J.F. (Journal Folio):** Column in Ledger pointing to the Journal page.
- **T-Shaped Account:** The standard format of a Ledger account.
- **Reliability Rule:** Journal > Ledger (because Journal is backed directly by Source Documents).
