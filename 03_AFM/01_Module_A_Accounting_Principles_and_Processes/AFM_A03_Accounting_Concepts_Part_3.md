## 1. Full Disclosure Concept

**Core Principle:**
A business must fully and completely disclose all material and relevant facts regarding its financial performance. Nothing should be hidden from stakeholders.

**Where is it disclosed?**
- In the Financial Statements (P&L, Balance Sheet).
- In the **Footnotes** (Notes to Accounts) for extra information (like pending court cases).

*Note: Regulatory bodies like SEBI and the Ministry of Corporate Affairs oversee businesses to ensure they comply with full disclosure.*

---

## 2. Materiality Concept

**Core Principle:**
This acts as a filter (and a slight contradiction) to Full Disclosure. It states that you should only separately disclose facts that are **materially important**. Immaterial items can be merged or treated simply.

**What makes a fact "Material"?**
Information is material if it can **influence the decision** of the users of the financial statements (investors, creditors, management). 

**Factors deciding Materiality:**
1. **Size** of the business.
2. **Nature** of the item.

*Examples:*
- **Immaterial:** Reliance Industries buys a ₹100 pen. It is recorded, but merged into "Stationery Expenses," not shown as a separate fixed asset because ₹100 won't change an investor's decision about Reliance.
- **Material:** A ₹500 Crore lawsuit is filed against Reliance. This is highly material and must be disclosed in the footnotes.

## 3. Prudence Concept (Conservatism / Playing Safe)

**Core Principle:**
"Anticipate no profit, but provide for all possible losses." 
The business should always play it safe.

**Key Rules:**
- **Future Losses:** If you anticipate a future loss, record it **today**. 
  - *Example:* Creating a **Provision for Doubtful Debts** if you suspect a debtor might go insolvent and not pay you.
- **Future Profits:** If you anticipate a future profit (e.g., you are winning a pending court case), do **NOT** record it until the gain is actually realized/received.

**Major Applications:**
- **Inventory Valuation:** Inventory is always valued at **Cost Price or Market Price, whichever is lower**.
- **Provisions:** Creating provisions for doubtful debts or upcoming expenses.

> **MCQ Trap:** Prudence does **NOT** mean deliberately undervaluing assets to create "Secret Reserves". It simply means preparing for likely losses and not overestimating profits.

---

## 4. Cost-Benefit Concept

**Core Principle:**
The cost incurred to provide any accounting information should **not exceed the benefit** derived from that information.

- *Example:* A company hires Big 4 auditors and spends ₹50,000 on an extensive 50-page audit report just to track a missing ₹100 stapler. The cost of the information (₹50k) far exceeds the benefit (₹100). This violates the Cost-Benefit concept.

---

## 5. Objectivity Concept

**Core Principle:**
All accounting transactions must be recorded in an **objective manner**, free from the personal bias, assumptions, or guesswork of the accountant.

- **How is this achieved?** Every single transaction must be supported by a **verifiable source document** (e.g., an invoice, bill, cash memo, or voucher). 
- Anecdotal evidence or hearsay ("trust me bro") is strictly not allowed in accounting.

## MCQs Solved in the Video

**Q1.** Consider the following statements regarding the Materiality Concept:
1. Materiality depends upon the size and the nature of the item.
2. Every transaction, irrespective of its significance, must receive equal accounting treatment.
3. Material facts are those that can influence the decision of the users of the financial statement.
Which statements are correct?
**Answer: Statements 1 and 3 are correct.**
*Explanation:* Statement 2 is false. Materiality dictates that significant (material) items are treated differently than insignificant (immaterial) ones.

**Q2.** A company provides for doubtful debts, maintains documentary evidence for transactions, and discloses pending litigation in the Notes to Accounts. Which accounting concepts is the company applying?
**Answer: Prudence, Objectivity, and Full Disclosure.**
*Explanation:* 
- Doubtful debts = Prudence
- Documentary evidence = Objectivity
- Disclosing pending litigation = Full Disclosure

**Q3.** A company decides not to generate an expensive internal report because the cost of providing the information exceeds the likely benefits to the users. Which concept is applied?
**Answer: Cost-Benefit Concept.**
*Explanation:* Self-explanatory; the cost outweighs the benefit.

## Quick-Fire Revision

- **Full Disclosure:** Disclose all relevant financial facts completely (use Footnotes).
- **Materiality:** Only facts that can change a user's decision are material. Depends on the item's nature and the business's size.
- **Prudence (Conservatism):** Record future losses today (Provisions). Ignore future profits until realized. Inventory valued at Cost or Market Price (whichever is lower).
- **Cost-Benefit:** Don't spend ₹1000 to track ₹10. Cost of information must be less than its benefit.
- **Objectivity:** Every entry needs a verifiable proof (bill/invoice). No personal bias or guesswork.
