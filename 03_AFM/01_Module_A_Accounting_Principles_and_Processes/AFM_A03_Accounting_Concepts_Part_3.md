## 1. Full Disclosure Concept

**The Core Rule:** A business must fully and completely disclose all **material and relevant facts** regarding its financial performance. Nothing should be hidden from stakeholders.

**Location of Disclosure:**
- Within the main Financial Statements (P&L, Balance Sheet).
- In the **Footnotes (Notes to Accounts)** for extra information like pending litigation.

**Regulatory Oversight:** Bodies like **SEBI** and the **Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA)** enforce this.

---

## 2. Materiality Concept

**The Core Rule:** Only disclose facts that are **materially important**. (This acts as a practical filter to the Full Disclosure Concept).

**What makes a fact "Material"?**
Information is material ONLY IF it can **influence the decision of the users** of the financial statements.

**Deciding Factors:**
1. **Size** of the business.
2. **Nature** of the item.

> **Case Scenario (Material vs. Immaterial):** 
> - **Immaterial:** Reliance Industries buys a ₹100 pen. It is recorded but merged into general "Stationery Expenses," not shown as a separate fixed asset because ₹100 won't change an investor's decision.
> - **Material:** A ₹500 Crore lawsuit is filed against Reliance. This is highly material and **must be disclosed in the footnotes**.
> - *Note:* For a very small business with ₹1000 in total sales, a ₹100 pen *might* be material. It is relative.

> **MCQ Trap:** "Every transaction, irrespective of its significance, must receive equal accounting treatment." -> **FALSE**. Materiality dictates that significant items are treated differently than insignificant ones.

## 3. Prudence Concept (Conservatism / Playing Safe)

**The Core Rule:** "Anticipate **NO profit**, but provide for **ALL possible losses**." The business should always play it safe.

**Strict Applications:**
- **Future Losses:** If you anticipate a future loss, record it **today**.
  - > **Case Scenario:** If you suspect a debtor might go insolvent, you immediately create a **Provision for Doubtful Debts**.
- **Future Profits:** If you anticipate a future profit (e.g., winning a pending court case), do **NOT** record it until the gain is actually realized.
- **Inventory Valuation:** Always valued at **Cost Price or Market Price, whichever is lower**.

> **MCQ Trap ("Secret Reserves"):** Prudence does **NOT** mean deliberately undervaluing assets to hide profits and create "Secret Reserves". It strictly means preparing for likely losses and not overestimating profits.

---

## 4. Cost-Benefit Concept

**The Core Rule:** The cost incurred to provide any accounting information should **not exceed the benefit** derived from that information.

> **Case Scenario:** A company hires Big 4 auditors and spends ₹50,000 on an extensive 50-page audit report just to track a missing ₹100 stapler. The cost of the information (₹50k) far exceeds the benefit (₹100). This violates the Cost-Benefit concept.

---

## 5. Objectivity Concept

**The Core Rule:** All accounting transactions must be recorded in an **objective manner**, completely free from the personal bias, assumptions, or guesswork of the accountant.

**The Requirement:**
Every single transaction MUST be supported by a **verifiable source document** (e.g., an invoice, bill, cash memo, or voucher). 

> **MCQ Trap:** "Anecdotal evidence or personal estimates can be used to record assets if no invoice is available." -> **FALSE**. Only verifiable documentary evidence is allowed.

## MCQs Solved in the Video

**Q1.** Consider the following statements regarding the Materiality Concept:
1. Materiality depends upon the size and the nature of the item.
2. Every transaction, irrespective of its significance, must receive equal accounting treatment.
3. Material facts are those that can influence the decision of the users of the financial statement.
**Answer: Statements 1 and 3 are correct.**
*Why Option 2 is wrong:* Materiality explicitly requires treating significant (material) items differently from insignificant (immaterial) ones.

**Q2.** A company provides for doubtful debts, maintains documentary evidence for transactions, and discloses pending litigation in the Notes to Accounts. Which accounting concepts is the company applying?
**Answer: Prudence, Objectivity, and Full Disclosure.**
*Keyword Mapping:* 
- Doubtful debts = **Prudence**
- Documentary evidence = **Objectivity**
- Disclosing pending litigation = **Full Disclosure**

**Q3.** A company decides not to generate an expensive internal report because the cost of providing the information exceeds the likely benefits to the users. Which concept is applied?
**Answer: Cost-Benefit Concept.**

## Quick-Fire Revision

- **Full Disclosure:** Disclose all relevant financial facts completely (use **Footnotes**).
- **Materiality:** Only facts that can **influence a user's decision** are material. Depends on the item's **nature** and the business's **size**.
- **Prudence (Conservatism):** Record future losses today (**Provisions**). Ignore future profits until realized. Inventory = **Lower of Cost or Market Price**.
- **Cost-Benefit:** Cost of information must be less than its benefit.
- **Objectivity:** Every entry needs a **verifiable source document**. No personal bias.
