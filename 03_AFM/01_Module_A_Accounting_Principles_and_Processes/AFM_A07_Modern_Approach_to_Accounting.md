## The Modern Approach (The "CREAL" Rule)

Unlike the Traditional Approach (Golden Rules), the **Modern Approach** classifies all accounts into strictly **5 categories** using the acronym **C.R.E.A.L.**

*Note: Regardless of whether you use the Traditional or Modern approach, the final Journal Entry will always be exactly the same.*

### 1. C - Capital
- The money invested by the owner into the business. 
- Due to the **Business Entity Concept**, Capital is treated as an **Internal Liability** for the business.
- **Drawings:** Money withdrawn by the owner for personal use (this decreases the Capital/Liability).

### 2. R - Revenue
- Income earned by the business from its normal operations.
- Examples: Sales, Rent Received, Interest Received, Dividend Income, and **Professional Income** (fees earned by a CA/Doctor/Lawyer).

### 3. E - Expenses
- The money going out to run business operations.
- Examples: Salaries, Rent Paid, Utility bills (electricity/water), Postages, Taxes.
> **MCQ Trap (Depreciation):** Depreciation is heavily tested. It is strictly an **Expense**, specifically a **Non-Cash Expense** (it reduces asset value due to wear and tear without cash leaving the business).

### 4. A - Assets
- The resources and properties owned by the business.
- **Classification of Assets:**
  1. **By Convertibility:** 
     - *Current Assets:* Converts to cash in **< 1 year** (e.g., Debtors paying in 3 months). 
     - *Fixed Assets:* Takes **> 1 year** to convert (e.g., Machinery).
  2. **By Physical Existence:** 
     - *Tangible:* Can touch/see (Furniture, ATMs).
     - *Intangible:* Cannot touch/see (Patents, Copyrights, Trademarks, Goodwill).
  3. **By Usage:** 
     - *Operating:* Used in routine business.
     - *Non-Operating:* Owned but not used regularly.

### 5. L - Liabilities
- Financial obligations payable to outsiders (External Liabilities).
- Examples: Bank Loans, Taxes Payable, Accounts Payable.
> **MCQ Trap (Unearned Revenue):** "Unearned Revenue" or "Income Received in Advance" is NOT a revenue account; it is strictly a **Liability**. Why? Because you have received the money but owe the service/product to the customer.

## The Rules of Debit and Credit (Modern Approach)

To memorize the Modern Rules, split the 5 categories into two groups:

### Group 1: Assets & Expenses (A & E)
> **Rule:** 
> **Increase (↑) = DEBIT**
> **Decrease (↓) = CREDIT**

### Group 2: Capital, Revenue, & Liabilities (C, R, L)
> **Rule:** 
> **Increase (↑) = CREDIT**
> **Decrease (↓) = DEBIT**
*(Notice that this is the exact opposite of Group 1).*

## Practical Journal Entries (Applying the Rules)

> **Case Scenario (Owner Invests Capital):** The Proprietor starts the business with Cash.
> - Cash (Asset) is increasing ↑ → **Debit Cash Account**.
> - Capital (Liability) is increasing ↑ → **Credit Capital Account**.
> *Entry:* Cash A/c Dr. To Capital A/c.

> **Case Scenario (Paying an Expense by Cheque):** Paid monthly salaries of ₹50,000 by Cheque.
> - Salary (Expense) is increasing ↑ → **Debit Salary Account**.
> - Bank (Asset) is decreasing ↓ → **Credit Bank Account**.
> *Entry:* Salary A/c Dr. To Bank A/c.

> **Case Scenario (Partial Payment for Asset):** Bought Machinery for ₹1 Lakh from Steel Ltd. Paid ₹40,000 cash immediately, balance later.
> - Machinery (Asset) is increasing by 1L ↑ → **Debit Machinery ₹1,00,000**.
> - Cash (Asset) is decreasing by 40k ↓ → **Credit Cash ₹40,000**.
> - Steel Ltd (Liability) is increasing by 60k ↑ → **Credit Steel Ltd ₹60,000**.

## MCQs Solved in the Video

**Q1.** A company received a commission of ₹40,000 directly into its bank account. Which accounts will be affected?
**Answer: A Revenue Account and an Asset Account.**
*Explanation:* Commission Received = Revenue Account. Bank = Asset Account.

**Q2.** A company purchased machinery worth ₹8 Lakh by paying ₹3 Lakh immediately in cash and agreeing to pay the balance later. Which accounts will be **credited**?
**Answer: The Cash Account and the Supplier Account.**
*Keyword Mapping:*
- Cash (Asset) decreases ↓ = Credit.
- Supplier (Liability) increases ↑ = Credit.
- Machinery (Asset) increases ↑ = Debit (The question specifically asked what will be *Credited*).

**Q3.** Which one of the following combinations correctly represents the **Debit Rule** under the Modern Approach?
(A) Increase in Asset and Increase in Expense
(B) Increase in Revenue and Increase in Capital
(C) Increase in Liability and Decrease in Revenue
(D) Increase in Asset and Decrease in Liability
**Answer: (A) Increase in Asset and Increase in Expense.**
*Why others are wrong:* According to the CREAL rule, an increase in Revenue, Capital, or Liability is always **Credited**, not Debited.

## Quick-Fire Revision

- **Modern Approach Acronym:** **C.R.E.A.L** (Capital, Revenue, Expenses, Assets, Liabilities).
- **Rule for Assets & Expenses:** Increase = Debit | Decrease = Credit.
- **Rule for Capital, Revenue, Liabilities:** Increase = Credit | Decrease = Debit.
- **Capital:** Treated as an Internal Liability.
- **Unearned Revenue:** Treated as a Liability (money received, service pending).
- **Depreciation:** Treated as an Expense (specifically Non-Cash).
- **Current vs Fixed Assets:** Divided by the **1-year** convertibility rule.
