## 1. Going Concern Concept

**Core Principle:** 
A business is established with the intention to run for a long period in the future. There is no intention to liquidate or close the business in the short term. The business is assumed to be "immortal."

**Key Implications & Accounting Treatments:**
- **Fixed Assets Valuation:** Assets are recorded at their **Historical Cost** (purchase price minus depreciation), not at their Current Market Value. 
  - *Example:* If a machine bought for ₹2 Lakhs becomes obsolete and its market value drops to ₹20,000, we still record it at cost minus depreciation, because we intend to use it, not sell it immediately.
- **Depreciation:** The cost of an asset is divided over its entire useful life rather than expensed in one year.
- **Provisions for Future:** We create provisions today for future liabilities (e.g., Provision for Doubtful Debts) because we assume the business will exist in the future to face those liabilities.
- **Deferred Revenue Expenditures:** Huge expenses that give benefits over multiple years (e.g., heavy advertising expense of ₹8 Lakhs giving benefits for 4 years) are not written off in one year. They are spread across multiple years (₹2 Lakhs per year).

> **MCQ Trap:** If a question asks why fixed assets are NOT shown at market value, or why heavy preliminary/advertising expenses are spread over several years, the answer is always the **Going Concern Concept**.

## 2. Consistency Concept

**Core Principle:** 
Accounting policies and practices must be applied uniformly and consistently year-on-year. 

- **Why?** It allows for easy **comparability** of financial statements. You can compare your own business's performance from 2026 to 2027 (intra-firm), or compare your business with another business (inter-firm).
- *Example:* If a business uses the Straight Line Method (SLM) for depreciation, it should continue using SLM every year. It shouldn't randomly switch to the Written Down Value (WDV) method.

**Can policies be changed?**
Yes, changes in accounting policies are allowed. However, any change **must be clearly disclosed** and explained in the financial statements (why the change was made).

## 3. Accrual Basis vs. Cash Basis of Accounting

### Accrual Basis (The Standard Method)
- **Income:** Recorded when it is **earned**, regardless of when cash is received.
- **Expense:** Recorded when it is **incurred**, regardless of when cash is paid.
- **Timing of Cash:** Irrelevant. The transaction date is what matters.

### Cash Basis
- **Income/Expense:** Recorded ONLY when cash actually comes in or goes out.

### The 10th June vs 20th June Example:
- **Scenario:** Goods sold on credit on 10th June for ₹30,000. Cash received on 20th June.
- **Accrual Method:** Records the sale on **10th June**.
- **Cash Method:** Records the sale on **20th June**.

### Why Accrual is Superior (Comparison)

| Feature | Accrual Basis | Cash Basis |
|---------|---------------|------------|
| **Accuracy** | More accurate, presents real picture | Less accurate, can be misleading (e.g., showing 0 sales mid-month despite actual sales happening on credit) |
| **Approval** | Approved by GAAP and Taxation Authorities | **NOT** approved by GAAP or Taxation Authorities |
| **Suitability** | Suitable for large organizations | Suitable only for very small businesses |
| **Trend Analysis** | Highly suitable for setting trends | Not suitable |
| **Maintenance** | Complex (requires Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable, etc.) | Simple |

## MCQs Solved in the Video

**Q1.** A business incurred heavy preliminary expenses while setting up operations. Instead of charging the entire amount to the current year's Profit & Loss account, it writes off the expenditure over several years. Which concept justifies this?
**Answer: Going Concern Concept**
*Explanation:* Because the business assumes it will run for a long time, it can spread the expense over the future years that will receive the benefit.

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding the Consistency Concept:
1. Accounting policies must be applied uniformly.
2. Changes in accounting policies need not be disclosed.
3. Consistency improves the comparability of financial statements.
Which statements are correct?
**Answer: 1 and 3 are correct.**
*Explanation:* Statement 2 is false. Any change in accounting policy MUST be properly disclosed.

**Q3.** A consulting firm completed a project in March 2026 and received payment in April 2026. Under System 1, the income was recorded in March itself. Under System 2, it was recorded in April. What are these systems?
**Answer: System 1 = Accrual Basis, System 2 = Cash Basis**
*Explanation:* March (when earned) = Accrual. April (when cash received) = Cash.

## Quick-Fire Revision

- **Going Concern:** Business will run forever. Assets recorded at cost (not market value). Deferred revenue expenditures spread over years. Provisions created for future.
- **Consistency:** Same accounting policies year-on-year. Enables comparability. Changes allowed but MUST be disclosed.
- **Accrual Basis:** Record when earned/incurred. Timing of cash is irrelevant. Approved by GAAP/Tax authorities. Good for trend analysis.
- **Cash Basis:** Record only on cash movement. Not approved by GAAP. Misleading for mid-period assessments. Simple but less accurate.
