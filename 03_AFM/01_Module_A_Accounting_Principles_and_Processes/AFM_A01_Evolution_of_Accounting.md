## The Journey: Record Keeping to Strategic Decision Making

Accounting did not start in its current form. It evolved in stages.

---

## Stage 1 — Ancient Origins

**Luca de Bargo Pacioli (Italy)**
- In **1494**, he wrote a book called **Summa**
- In this book, he mentioned **double entry bookkeeping for the first time**
- He is known as the **Father of Accounting**

> MCQ Trap: The question may describe the book Summa or say "father of accounting" and ask you to identify the person. Answer = Luca Pacioli.

**Ancient India**
- **Kautilya (Chanakya)** mentioned accounting-related transactions in his book **Arthashastra**
- Ancient Indian accounting systems that developed: **Nama, Mahajani, Marwari, Desi**

---

## Stage 2 — Stewardship Accounting

- Rich aristocrats had lots of assets (property, cash, ornaments) but did not know how to record them
- They appointed **stewards** to do record keeping on their behalf
- Stewards managed books of accounts and assets for the owners
- This is **Stewardship Accounting** — managing someone else's assets and reporting back to the owner

---

## Stage 3 — Financial Accounting

- As industrialisation came, businesses expanded in size
- Owners alone could not fund businesses — **investors** (shareholders) came in
- These investors + other parties (government, trade unions, creditors, employees, customers) all needed financial information
- This need gave rise to **Financial Accounting**
- Financial Accounting = recording, classifying and summarising transactions (produces P&L, Balance Sheet)
- Strict **accounting standards** were followed
- Purpose: Provide **reliable, sufficient, timely** financial information to all stakeholders

---

## Stage 4 — Cost Accounting

- After knowing overall profit/loss, businessmen asked: **how much does it cost to make one unit?**
- Example (Pepsi, 1 litre bottle): costs include:
  - Raw material (liquid, sugar, etc.)
  - Labour cost
  - Factory overheads (electricity, rent, machinery depreciation)
  - Admin / office expenses
  - Selling costs
- Sum of all = **cost per unit** → add profit margin → get **selling price**
- This is **Cost Accounting**

---

## Stage 5 — Management Accounting

- Management needed financial information for **internal decision making about the future**
- Financial Accounting is for outsiders (investors), follows strict rules
- Management Accounting is for **insiders (management)** — flexible rules, no external submission needed
- Focus = **forward-looking** (also called **Forward Accounting**)
- Uses: budgets, forecasts, deciding whether to launch a new product line

| | Financial Accounting | Management Accounting |
|--|--|--|
| For whom | External stakeholders | Internal management |
| Rules | Strict (accounting standards) | Flexible |
| Focus | Past transactions | Future decisions |
| Output | P&L, Balance Sheet | Reports, budgets, forecasts |

---

## Stage 6 — Modern Forms of Accounting

### Social Responsibility Accounting
- Business has responsibilities towards society (CSR)
- Records money spent on: education, poverty reduction, sanitation, hygiene maintenance

### Human Resource Accounting
- Employees = human resources = valuable but hard to measure in money
- **Roger H. Hermanson** wrote about it in **1964**, book = **Human Capital**
- **A. Sorey** mentioned it in **Accounting for Human Assets**
- What is recorded: recruitment ad cost, interview cost, training and development costs
- All costs incurred to hire and develop an employee are recorded under HRA

> MCQ Trap: Who wrote about HRA? Answer = **Roger H. Hermanson (1964)**. Book = **Human Capital**.

### Value Accounting
- Measures the **contribution of an individual to their department**

### Fair Value Accounting (= Current Value / Current Market Value Accounting)
- Record assets at **current market value** instead of historical cost
- Approaches: **Market approach, Income approach, Cost approach**
- **Did NOT get wide acceptance** because:
  - High cost of calculating fair value
  - Market values keep fluctuating — not reliable
  - No verifiable source document — violates the objectivity principle
- Fair Value = Current Market Value (remember this equation)

## MCQs Solved in the Video

**Q1.** A manufacturing company calculates the cost of producing each unit. Management then uses this data along with budgets and forecasts to decide whether a new product line should be launched. Which two types of accounting are involved?

Options: A) Financial and Cost  B) Cost and Financial  C) Cost and Management  D) Management and Social Responsibility

**Answer: C — Cost Accounting and Management Accounting**
Calculating unit cost = Cost Accounting. Using that + forecasts for future decisions = Management Accounting.

---

**Q2.** A student reads a book called Summa published in 1494, which first mentioned double entry bookkeeping. Who is the author, also known as the Father of Accounting?

**Answer: Luca Pacioli**
Watch for the twist — question describes the book/year without naming the person directly.

---

**Q3.** A company estimates the value contributed by its employees and records all investments made in recruitment, training and development. Which type of accounting is this?

**Answer: Human Resource Accounting**
Written about by **Roger H. Hermanson** in **1964** in his book **Human Capital**.

## Quick-Fire Revision

- Father of Accounting = **Luca Pacioli** | Book = **Summa** | Year = **1494** | First mentioned double entry bookkeeping
- Ancient India = **Kautilya (Chanakya)** | Book = **Arthashastra**
- Ancient Indian styles: **Nama, Mahajani, Marwari, Desi**
- **Stewardship Accounting** = stewards managing assets on behalf of rich owners
- **Financial Accounting** = for external stakeholders, strict rules, P&L + Balance Sheet
- **Cost Accounting** = calculates cost per unit = raw material + labour + overheads + admin + selling costs
- **Management Accounting** = internal, flexible, forward-looking, for decision making
- **Social Responsibility Accounting** = tracks CSR spend (education, poverty, sanitation)
- **Human Resource Accounting** = Roger H. Hermanson (1964), Human Capital | records recruitment + training + development costs
- **Value Accounting** = measures individual contribution to department
- **Fair Value Accounting** = current market value | NOT widely accepted | failed due to high cost + fluctuation + no reliable source document
- Evolution order: **Ancient origins → Stewardship → Financial → Cost → Management → Modern forms**
