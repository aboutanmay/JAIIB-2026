## The 3 Pillars of a Transaction
Before applying any rule, you must answer three questions:
1. **What is being exchanged?** (e.g., A Laptop)
2. **What is the medium of exchange?** (Cash or Credit/Udhār)
3. **Which parties are involved?** (e.g., Seema and Dell Company)

Under the **Traditional Approach** (also known as the Golden Rules of Accounting), all accounts are classified into three strict categories: **Personal**, **Real**, and **Nominal**.

---

## 1. Personal Accounts

Relates to persons (individuals, firms, companies).

**Three Sub-Categories:**
1. **Natural Persons:** Created by God. (e.g., Ram, Seema, Owner, Supplier).
2. **Artificial Persons:** Created by Law/Man. They act as separate legal entities. (e.g., ICICI Bank, SBI, Reliance Industries, Schools, Clubs, Companies).
3. **Representative Persons:** Accounts that represent a group of natural/artificial persons. (e.g., Capital Account, Drawings Account, Outstanding Salary, Prepaid Rent).

> **The Golden Rule for Personal Accounts:**
> **DEBIT the Receiver**
> **CREDIT the Giver**

> **Case Scenario (Credit Purchase):** You purchased goods from ABC Ltd on credit. ABC Ltd is an Artificial Person. ABC Ltd is *giving* you the goods. Since they are the giver, ABC Ltd's account will be **Credited**.

---

## 2. Real Accounts

Relates to **Assets** of the business.

**Two Sub-Categories:**
1. **Tangible Assets:** Can be seen and touched. (e.g., Cash, Machinery, Plant, Inventory, Land).
2. **Intangible Assets:** Cannot be seen or touched but hold value. (e.g., Patents, Copyrights, Goodwill, Trademarks, Brand Reputation).

> **The Golden Rule for Real Accounts:**
> **DEBIT what comes in**
> **CREDIT what goes out**

> **Case Scenario (Asset Purchase for Cash):** Ram purchased furniture for ₹1,20,000 in cash. 
> - Furniture (Asset/Real) comes into the business → **Debit Furniture Account**.
> - Cash (Asset/Real) goes out of the business → **Credit Cash Account**.

---

## 3. Nominal Accounts

Relates strictly to **Expenses, Losses, Incomes, and Gains**.

- **Expenses/Losses:** Salaries, Wages, Rent Paid, Discount Allowed, Loss by Fire, Purchases.
- **Incomes/Gains:** Sales, Discount Received, Commission Received, Rent Received.

> **The Golden Rule for Nominal Accounts:**
> **DEBIT all Expenses and Losses**
> **CREDIT all Incomes and Gains**

> **Case Scenario (Paying an Expense):** Paid Rent ₹300 in cash.
> - Rent is an expense (Nominal) → **Debit Rent Account**.
> - Cash is going out (Real) → **Credit Cash Account**.

## Critical Rules & MCQ Traps

> **MCQ Trap (Prefix/Suffix on Nominal Accounts):** 
> By default, "Rent" or "Salary" is a Nominal Account. However, if you add a prefix or suffix (like *Outstanding*, *Prepaid*, *Accrued*, or *Advance*), it completely changes category!
> - Salary Account = **Nominal Account**
> - *Outstanding* Salary Account = **Representative Personal Account**
> - *Prepaid* Insurance Account = **Representative Personal Account**

> **MCQ Trap ("Purchases" vs Asset Name):** 
> The words **"Purchases"** and **"Sales"** are strictly reserved ONLY for the purchase/sale of **Goods** (the items a business trades in). 
> If a business buys an Asset (like Furniture, Machinery, or a Car), you MUST use the Asset's name.
> - Bought goods for cash → Debit Purchases.
> - Bought furniture for cash → **Debit Furniture** (Do NOT debit Purchases).

## MCQs Solved in the Video

**Q1.** Furniture worth ₹80,000 was purchased on credit from Modern Furnitures. What is the correct journal entry?
**Answer:** Debit Furniture Account | Credit Modern Furnitures Account.
*Keyword Mapping / Logic:* 
- Furniture is coming in (Real Account) → Debit.
- Modern Furnitures is the Giver (Personal Account) → Credit.
- *Why not Debit Purchases?* Because Furniture is an asset, not "goods."

**Q2.** Match the transaction to the correct Debit/Credit action:
1. Earned an income
2. Bought a business asset
3. Disposed of a business asset
4. Incurred an expense
**Answer:**
1. Earned Income = **Credit** (Nominal Rule: Credit all incomes & gains)
2. Bought Asset = **Debit** (Real Rule: Debit what comes in)
3. Disposed Asset = **Credit** (Real Rule: Credit what goes out)
4. Incurred Expense = **Debit** (Nominal Rule: Debit all expenses)

**Q3.** A business has the following three accounts: Machinery, Rent Received, and Prepaid Insurance. Classify them into Real, Nominal, or Personal.
**Answer:**
- Machinery = **Real Account** (It is a tangible asset).
- Rent Received = **Nominal Account** (It is an income).
- Prepaid Insurance = **Representative Personal Account**.
*Why Option C (Prepaid Insurance) is the trap:* Insurance alone is Nominal, but adding "Prepaid" makes it Representative Personal.

## Quick-Fire Revision

- **Personal Accounts:** Natural, Artificial (Companies/Banks), Representative (Capital/Drawings/Outstanding).
  - *Rule:* **Debit Receiver | Credit Giver**.
- **Real Accounts:** Assets (Tangible & Intangible like Goodwill/Patents).
  - *Rule:* **Debit what comes in | Credit what goes out**.
- **Nominal Accounts:** Expenses, Losses, Incomes, Gains.
  - *Rule:* **Debit Expenses/Losses | Credit Incomes/Gains**.
- **The Capital Rule:** When the owner invests cash, Debit Cash (Real), Credit Capital (Personal - Giver).
- **The "Prepaid/Outstanding" Exception:** Adding these words to a Nominal account turns it into a **Representative Personal** account.
- **Goods vs Assets:** Only use "Purchases/Sales" for goods. Use the specific name for assets.
