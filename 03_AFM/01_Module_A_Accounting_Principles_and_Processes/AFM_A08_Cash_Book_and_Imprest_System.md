## 1. The Cash Book: Basics & Types

**The Core Rule:** The Cash Book only records **Cash and Bank transactions**. 
- **Debit Side (Left):** Records all Receipts (money coming in).
- **Credit Side (Right):** Records all Payments (money going out).

> **MCQ Trap (Dual Nature):** The Cash Book serves as BOTH the **Book of Original Entry** (because transactions are recorded here chronologically first) AND a **Ledger Account** (because it is T-shaped, eliminating the need to open a separate Cash Account in the ledger).

**Types of Cash Books:**
1. **Single Column:** Has only one amount column (Cash).
2. **Double Column:** Has two amount columns (Cash & Discount OR Cash & Bank).
3. **Triple Column:** Has three amount columns (Cash, Bank, and Discount).

---

## 2. The Contra Entry (Highly Tested)

**The Core Rule:** A **Contra Entry** is a transaction where BOTH the debit and credit aspects are recorded in the *same* Cash Book (specifically in Double or Triple column cash books).
- It is denoted by writing a **"C"** in the Ledger Folio (L.F.) column.

**Valid Contra Entries:**
1. **Cash deposited into Bank:** (Bank balance ↑ Debit | Cash balance ↓ Credit).
2. **Cash withdrawn from Bank for *Office/Business* Use:** (Cash balance ↑ Debit | Bank balance ↓ Credit).

> **MCQ Trap ("NOT" a Contra Entry):** 
> Withdrawing cash from the bank for **Personal Use** is NEVER a contra entry! It is considered **Drawings**. It only appears on the Credit (payment) side of the Bank column and goes to the Drawings ledger account.

---

## 3. Balancing Rules for the Cash Book

At the end of the period, the Cash Book must be balanced. However, strict rules apply to different columns:

- **Cash Column:** Can **ONLY have a Debit Balance** (or zero). It can *never* have a credit balance because you cannot spend more physical cash than you possess. 
  - *Note:* A Debit Balance means Receipts > Payments (a Favorable Balance).
- **Bank Column:** Can have a Debit Balance (Favorable) OR a Credit Balance (which indicates a Bank Overdraft).

> **MCQ Trap (The Discount Column):**
> The Discount column is **NEVER BALANCED**. You simply total the two sides separately. 
> *Why?* Because Discount is a **Nominal Account**. Nominal accounts are never balanced; their totals are transferred directly to the Profit & Loss Account.

## 4. Petty Cash Book & The Imprest System

**The Core Rule:** A Petty Cash Book is used to record small, routine expenses (e.g., postage, stationery, tea/coffee, auto fare) to prevent overburdening the Head Cashier. It is maintained by a **Petty Cashier**.

> **Case Scenario (The Imprest System):** 
> Reliance gives its Petty Cashier a fixed advance (Imprest amount) of ₹30,000 for a 30-day period. During the month, the Petty Cashier spends ₹28,200 on small expenses. 
> At the end of the month, the Petty Cashier submits the record. The Head Cashier will reimburse **exactly ₹28,200** to the Petty Cashier. 
> *Result:* The starting balance for the next month is instantly restored to the original fixed ₹30,000.

**Benefits of the Imprest System:**
- Saves the Head Cashier's time.
- Better control over small cash leakages and reduced chances of fraud.

## MCQs Solved in the Video

**Q1.** Which of the following is **NOT** a Contra Entry?
(A) Cash withdrawn from Bank.
(B) Cash deposited into Bank.
(C) Cash withdrawn from Bank for personal use.
**Answer: (C) Cash withdrawn from bank for personal use.**
*Why Option C is wrong:* Personal use withdrawals are "Drawings" and only affect one side of the cash book. They are not contra entries.

**Q2.** In a two-column cash book, "Cash deposited into Bank" is recorded as:
**Answer: Debited in the Bank column (and Credited in the Cash column).**
*Keyword Mapping:* Bank is receiving money (Asset ↑ = Debit). Cash is going out (Asset ↓ = Credit).

**Q3.** A debit balance in the Cash Book represents:
1. A favorable balance.
2. Excess of receipts over payments.
**Answer: Both statements are correct.**
*Explanation:* For assets like cash, Debit means positive (favorable) because more money came in (receipts) than went out (payments).

## Quick-Fire Revision

- **Cash Book Nature:** Both a Journal (Original Entry) AND a Ledger.
- **Contra Entry:** Affects both Cash and Bank columns simultaneously. Marked with **"C"**.
- **Personal Use Withdrawal:** NOT a contra entry (treated as Drawings).
- **Cash Column Balance:** ALWAYS Debit (or zero). Never Credit.
- **Discount Column:** NEVER balanced (because it's a Nominal Account).
- **Imprest System:** Petty cashier gets a fixed advance. Only the *exact amount spent* is reimbursed to restore the original limit.
